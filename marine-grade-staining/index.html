Stewart Bradley Pier Services / Marine-grade staining

Sun, moisture, and the waterways around Hampton Roads are hard on untreated wood.

A suitable finish will not make a tired pier new. It will slow the gray, help the boards shed water, and keep a freshly cleaned walkway looking like the work was worth doing. We stain after the surface is ready — not over dirt, algae, or wet wood.

Stewart Bradley Pier Services applies marine-grade staining on existing residential piers. Class A contractor, license #2705160892. Suffolk-based, including Virginia Beach waterfronts. If the boards are already failing, staining is the wrong spend; that conversation belongs on selective repairs or re-decking.

When staining belongs

Recently cleaned wood. A new re-deck that has dried. Boards that are sound but thirsty. Those are finish jobs. We do not stain over algae still on the surface, or on wood that should come up.

What the work includes

  • Confirm the wood is clean and dry enough to take a finish
  • Apply a stain suited to waterfront sun and moisture
  • Work the boards, edges, and reachable framing as the scope allows

This often follows pressure washing, and it can share a project with a re-deck or rope rail. You get a defined estimate first.

Hampton Roads exposure

The same sun and splash show up in Suffolk, Virginia Beach, Chesapeake, Smithfield, Norfolk, and Portsmouth — and on water like Lynnhaven, Broad Bay, Bennett’s Creek, Nansemond, and Western Branch. The finish has to be chosen for that, not for a backyard deck inland.

How it goes

A photo. An on-site visual. A written scope. We stain when the wood is ready.
