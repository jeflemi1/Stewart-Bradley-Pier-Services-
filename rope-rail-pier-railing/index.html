Stewart Bradley Pier Services / Rope rail

A two-course rope rail is the finish a lot of Hampton Roads piers are missing.

It gives you a handhold, a cleaner edge, and that traditional waterfront look. The work is the posts, the alignment, and the finishing — not just draping rope on a tired walkway and calling it done.

Stewart Bradley Pier Services installs traditional rope railing on existing residential piers. The featured project on the homepage is that work: renewed framing where it was needed, new decking, and a two-course rope rail that turned a weathered walkway into something you would actually use. Class A contractor, license #2705160892. Suffolk-based, including Virginia Beach and Chesapeake waterfronts.

When a rope rail belongs

A sound pier, or a pier already being re-decked. Posts that can be set in line. A homeowner who wants a classic waterfront railing rather than a heavy built-up system. Those are rail jobs.

If the structure is moving, connections have failed, or the pier needs more than a railing, we say so. We do not install new pilings. A rail will not hide a walkway that should come up; start with re-decking or selective repairs when that is the honest sequence.

What the work includes

  • Layout and post placement
  • Two-course rope rail
  • Alignment down the walkway
  • Careful finishing at the ends and posts

It often shares a visit with re-decking. You get a defined estimate first.

Where we install

Suffolk, Virginia Beach, Chesapeake, and Hampton Roads waterways including Lynnhaven, Broad Bay, Bennett’s Creek, Nansemond, and Western Branch. Access and the condition of the existing structure still decide whether the rail can go in on this visit.

How it goes

A photo. An on-site visual. A written scope. Then we install the agreed rail.
